## Context

An e-commerce business I founded and ran across two sales channels: a
marketplace, which carried the bulk of the volume, and my own storefront.

It began alongside a full-time engineering role and later became the full-time
one. I was the engineer and the owner, which meant the software and the business
answered to each other directly.

## Constraints

Sole engineer, sole owner. Sourcing, pricing, fulfilment and cash all landed on
me, alongside building and running the systems.

That is a harder constraint than it sounds. Engineering time was not budgeted
against a roadmap; it competed with everything else the business needed that
week, and lost whenever it should have.

## What I built

An internal inventory and order system that tracked stock across both channels,
flagged products for replenishment automatically, and generated sales invoices
through an accounting integration.

Deliberately internal and deliberately small. It existed to remove specific
recurring work, and its scope was whatever that came to.

## Decisions and trade-offs

**Operating inside marketplace constraints.** Listing rules, fulfilment SLAs and
channel-specific stock allocation were not negotiable inputs — the system had to
be shaped around them, including the parts I disagreed with. Allocation across
two channels sharing one pool of stock was the recurring difficulty.

**Building only what paid for itself.** Every piece of automation had to save
more than it cost to build and keep running. Plenty of things I wanted to build
did not clear that bar and were not built. This is the habit I brought back into
salaried work most usefully.

## Outcome

Peak annual turnover of R1.5m.

Wound down deliberately when margins no longer justified the effort — a decision
rather than a failure. It taught me more about trade-offs than any engineering
role has, because the feedback was immediate and financial.
