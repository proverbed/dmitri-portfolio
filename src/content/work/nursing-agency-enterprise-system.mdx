## Context

An enterprise system for a UK nursing agency, covering the full cycle: booking
candidates onto shifts, through scheduling, through to paying them.

A suite of products rather than one application, used daily by non-technical
staff whose work the system either supported or obstructed.

## Constraints

A compliance-sensitive domain with statutory pay and scheduling rules. Getting
either wrong has real consequences — for the agency, and for someone's pay
packet.

Rules of that kind are not requirements you implement once. They change outside
your control, and the system has to be shaped so that a change to a rule is a
change in one place.

## What I built

Backend functionality across a range of products in the agency's suite.

Database administration and web server maintenance — the operational side as
well as the development side.

Business reporting for non-technical users, so that answering a question about
the business did not require an engineer.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Requirements came directly from end users, and I translated them into functional
specifications working alongside the architect and product owner. The value in
that was in going back to the person who asked: what a user requests and what a
user needs are frequently different, and the difference only surfaces in
conversation.

## Outcome

Five and a half years on the system.

It was also my first experience leading other developers — specifying and
breaking down work for two engineers, which is a different skill from doing the
work and one I was not immediately good at.
